5  The Partial Autocorrelation Function (PACF)

5.1 Introduction

The autocorrelation function (ACF) measures the correlation between

\[ Y_t \]

and

\[ Y_{t-k}. \]

However, the autocorrelation at lag (k) may be influenced indirectly through intermediate lags.

The partial autocorrelation function (PACF) measures the direct relationship between

\[ Y_t \]

and

\[ Y_{t-k}, \]

after removing the linear effects of the intervening observations

\[ Y_{t-1},Y_{t-2},\ldots,Y_{t-k+1}. \]

The PACF is one of the most useful tools for identifying autoregressive models.

5.2 Motivation

Consider an AR(1) process

\[ Y_t=\phi Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t. \]

Since

\[ Y_t \]

depends directly only on

\[ Y_{t-1}, \]

any apparent correlation between

\[ Y_t \]

and

\[ Y_{t-2} \]

arises through

\[ Y_{t-1}. \]

The PACF removes this indirect effect.

5.3 Definition

The partial autocorrelation at lag (k), denoted by

\[ \alpha(k), \]

is the correlation between

\[ Y_t \]

and

\[ Y_{t-k} \]

after controlling for

\[ Y_{t-1},Y_{t-2},\ldots,Y_{t-k+1}. \]

Thus,

\[ \alpha(k) \]

measures the direct contribution of lag (k).

5.4 Computing the PACF

In practice the PACF is estimated using recursive regression methods or the Yule-Walker equations.

Fortunately, R computes it automatically.

set.seed(123)

y <- arima.sim(
  model=list(ar=.8),
  n=200
)

pacf(y)

5.5 PACF of White Noise

For white noise,

\[ Y_t \sim WN(0,\sigma^2), \]

there is no dependence between observations.

Therefore

\[ \alpha(k)=0, \qquad k\ge1. \]

5.5.1 Simulation

set.seed(123)

wn <- rnorm(200)

pacf(
  wn,
  main="PACF of White Noise"
)

All bars should fluctuate randomly around zero.

5.6 PACF of an AR(1) Process

Consider

\[ Y_t=\phi Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t, \]

with

\[ |\phi|<1. \]

The theoretical PACF is

\[ \alpha(1)=\phi, \]

and

\[ \alpha(k)=0, \qquad k>1. \]

Therefore, the PACF cuts off after lag 1.

5.6.1 Example

set.seed(123)

ar1 <- arima.sim(
  model=list(ar=.8),
  n=200
)

pacf(
  ar1,
  main="PACF of AR(1)"
)

A significant spike appears at lag 1, while later lags are close to zero.

5.7 PACF of an AR(2) Process

Consider

\[ Y_t = 0.7Y_{t-1} - 0.3Y_{t-2} + \varepsilon_t. \]

set.seed(123)

ar2 <- arima.sim(
  model=list(ar=c(.7,-.3)),
  n=500
)

pacf(
  ar2,
  main="PACF of AR(2)"
)

The PACF exhibits significant values at lags 1 and 2 and then cuts off.

5.8 PACF of an MA(1) Process

Consider

\[ Y_t=\varepsilon_t+\theta\varepsilon_{t-1}. \]

Unlike an AR process, the PACF does not cut off.

Instead, it decreases gradually toward zero.

5.8.1 Example

set.seed(123)

ma1 <- arima.sim(
  model=list(ma=.5),
  n=200
)

pacf(
  ma1,
  main="PACF of MA(1)"
)

The PACF tails off rather than terminating abruptly.

5.9 Comparison of ACF and PACF

5.9.1 AR Models

For an AR((p)) process:

  • ACF tails off.
  • PACF cuts off after lag (p).

5.9.2 MA Models

For an MA((q)) process:

  • ACF cuts off after lag (q).
  • PACF tails off.

5.9.3 ARMA Models

For an ARMA((p,q)) process:

  • ACF tails off.
  • PACF tails off.

Neither function exhibits a sharp cutoff.

5.10 Model Identification

The Box-Jenkins approach uses ACF and PACF together.

Model ACF PACF
AR(1) Tails off Cuts off after lag 1
AR(2) Tails off Cuts off after lag 2
MA(1) Cuts off after lag 1 Tails off
MA(2) Cuts off after lag 2 Tails off
ARMA(1,1) Tails off Tails off

These patterns are fundamental in practical model identification.

5.11 Example: Comparing AR and MA Models

par(mfrow=c(2,2))

ar1 <- arima.sim(model=list(ar=.8), n=300)
acf(ar1, main="AR(1): ACF")
pacf(ar1, main="AR(1): PACF")

ma1 <- arima.sim(model=list(ma=.8), n=300)
acf(ma1, main="MA(1): ACF")
pacf(ma1, main="MA(1): PACF")

Observe:

  • AR(1): ACF tails off, PACF cuts off.
  • MA(1): ACF cuts off, PACF tails off.

5.12 Statistical Significance

For large samples,

\[ SE(r_k) \approx \frac{1}{\sqrt{n}}. \]

The PACF plot displays approximate 95% confidence limits

\[ \pm\frac{1.96}{\sqrt n}. \]

Values outside these limits may indicate significant dependence.

5.13 Summary

  • The PACF measures direct dependence at a given lag.
  • PACF removes the effect of intermediate observations.
  • AR((p)) processes have PACFs that cut off after lag (p).
  • MA((q)) processes have PACFs that tail off.
  • ACF and PACF together are the primary tools for model identification.
